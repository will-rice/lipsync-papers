# Robust Unsupervised Audio-visual Speech Enhancement Using a Mixture of Variational Autoencoders

Mostafa Sadeghi    Xavier Alameda-Pineda    IEEE Senior Member
^(†)^(†)thanks: Xavier Alameda-Pineda acknowledges ANR and the IDEX for
funding the ML3RI project.

###### Abstract

Recently, an audio-visual speech generative model based on variational
autoencoder (VAE) has been proposed, which is combined with a NMF (NMF)
model for noise variance to perform unsupervised speech enhancement.
When visual data is clean, speech enhancement with audio-visual VAE
shows a better performance than with audio-only VAE, which is trained on
audio-only data. However, audio-visual VAE is not robust against noisy
visual data, e.g., when for some video frames, speaker face is not
frontal or lips region is occluded. In this paper, we propose a robust
unsupervised audio-visual speech enhancement method based on a per-frame
VAE mixture model. This mixture model consists of a trained audio-only
VAE and a trained audio-visual VAE. The motivation is to skip noisy
visual frames by switching to the audio-only VAE model. We present a
variational expectation-maximization method to estimate the parameters
of the model. Experiments show the promising performance of the proposed
method.

###### Index Terms: 

Robust audio-visual speech enhancement, generative models, variational
auto-encoder, mixture mode, variational expectation-maximization

## 1 Introduction

Speech enhancement – or how to estimate clean speech from a noisy signal
– has attracted a lot of attention, both for single- and multi-channel
audio recordings \[[1](#bib.bib1), [2](#bib.bib2), [3](#bib.bib3),
[4](#bib.bib4)\]. Recently, generative models have been utilized for
speech enhancement \[[5](#bib.bib5), [6](#bib.bib6), [7](#bib.bib7),
[8](#bib.bib8), [9](#bib.bib9), [10](#bib.bib10)\]. Specifically, some
works proposed to use variational autoencoder (VAE) to model speech
spectrogram, and then perform speech enhancement by considering an NMF
noise variance model \[[5](#bib.bib5), [6](#bib.bib6)\]. This is done in
an unsupervised way. A common characteristic of all these methods, which
we refer to as A-VAE (A-VAE), is the use of audio recordings only.

Audio-visual speech enhancement methods incorporate also the visual
information (video frames) associated with the noisy speech, aiming to
improve the quality of the enhanced speech signal \[[11](#bib.bib11),
[12](#bib.bib12), [13](#bib.bib13)\]. Using the video modality is
well-motivated by the fact that lips movements provide information about
what is being uttered. As an audio-visual extension of VAE-based methods
of \[[5](#bib.bib5), [6](#bib.bib6)\], an audio-visual VAE (AV-VAE)
model has recently been proposed in \[[14](#bib.bib14)\], training a VAE
model conditioned on visual features, e.g., lips ROI (ROI). For speech
enhancement, AV-VAE has been shown to outperform A-VAE specially in high
noise levels \[[14](#bib.bib14)\].

A critical problem with audio-visual methods is “noisy visual data”,
e.g., when speakers do not face the camera or the lips are occluded.
Specifically, the AV-VAE based speech enhancement method presented
in \[[14](#bib.bib14)\] uses clean visual data to train the speech
spectrogram prior. Therefore, it expects clean visual data as well in
the test (enhancement) phase for unseen data. Otherwise, its performance
could degrade below audio-only methods, as we will see later in this
paper.

The present work aims to provide a solution to the above-mentioned
problem. That is, to make AV-VAE speech enhancement robust against noisy
visual data. To achieve this goal, we propose a VAE mixture model
consisting of a trained A-VAE and a trained AV-VAE model. As said
before, AV-VAE yields poor results in the presence of noisy visual data.
However, the proposed mechanism would skip visual data whenever it is
not reliable, and uses the A-VAE model instead. Importantly, the choice
between A-VAE and AV-VAE is unsupervised and must be done for every
frame at test time. We present a variational inference framework to
tackle all these issues. Experimental results on clean as well as noisy
visual frames are provided, demonstrating the effectiveness of our
method.

## 2 VAE-based Speech Spectra Modeling

### 2.1 Audio-only VAE

In this section, we briefly review the VAE generative speech model that
was first proposed in \[[5](#bib.bib5)\]. Let $`s_{fn}`$ denote the
complex-valued speech STFT (STFT) coefficient at frequency index
$`f\in\{0,...,F-1\}`$ and at frame index $`n\in\{0,...,N-1\}`$. At each
TF (TF) bin, we have the following probabilistic generative model, which
will be referred to as A-VAE:

|     |                                 |                                                          |                                                                         |     |
| --- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle s\_{fn}         | \mathbf{z}\_{n}`$                                        | $`\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{N}_{c}(0,\sigma_{f}^{a}(\mathbf{z}_{n})),`$ |     | (1) |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{n}`$ | $`\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}),`$ |                                                                         | (2) |

where $`\mathbf{z}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{L}`$, with $`L\ll F`$, is a latent
random variable describing a speech generative process,
$`\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})`$ is a zero-mean multivariate
Gaussian distribution with identity covariance matrix, and
$`\mathcal{N}_{c}(0,\sigma)`$ is a univariate complex proper Gaussian
distribution with zero mean and variance $`\sigma`$. Let
$`\mathbf{s}_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{F}`$ be the vector whose components are
the speech STFT coefficients at frame $`n`$. The set of non-linear
functions
$`\{\sigma_{f}^{a}:\mathbb{R}^{L}\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{+}\}_{f=0}^{F-1}`$
are modeled as neural networks sharing the input
$`\mathbf{z}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{L}`$. These parameters are estimated
using variational inference by defining another neural network, called
encoder (inference) network, which approximates the intractable
posterior of $`{\bf z}_{n}`$ given $`{\bf s}_{n}`$ \[[15](#bib.bib15),
[5](#bib.bib5), [6](#bib.bib6)\].

### 2.2 Audio-visual VAE

In the AV-VAE framework proposed in \[[14](#bib.bib14)\], the following
latent space model is considered, independently for all
$`l\in\{0,...,L-1\}`$ and all TF bins $`(f,n)`$:

|     |                         |                                   |                                                                                                                  |     |
| --- | ----------------------- | --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle s\_{fn} | \mathbf{z}_{n},{\mathbf{v}}_{n}`$ | $`\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{N}_{c}(0,\sigma_{f}^{av}(\mathbf{z}_{n},{\mathbf{v}}_{n})),`$                        |     | (3) |
|     | $`\displaystyle z\_{ln} | {\mathbf{v}}\_{n}`$               | $`\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{N}\left(\bar{\mu}_{l}({\mathbf{v}}_{n}),\bar{\sigma}_{l}({\mathbf{v}}_{n})\right),`$ |     | (4) |

where $`{\mathbf{v}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}`$ is an embedding for the
image of the speaker lips at frame $`n`$, and the non-linear functions
$`\{{\sigma}_{f}^{av}:{\mathbb{R}}^{L}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{M}\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{+}\}_{f=0}^{F-1}`$
are modeled as a neural network taking $`{\bf z}_{n}`$ and
$`{\mathbf{v}}_{n}`$ as input. Furthermore, the non-linear functions
$`\{\bar{\mu}_{l}:{\mathbb{R}}^{M}\mapsto\mathbb{R}\}_{l=0}^{L-1}`$ and
$`\{\bar{\sigma}_{l}:{\mathbb{R}}^{M}\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{+}\}_{l=0}^{L-1}`$,
yielding $`{\bf z}_{n}`$’s prior, are collectively modeled with a neural
network which takes $`{\mathbf{v}}_{n}`$ as input. In a way similar to
A-VAE, an encoder network, approximating the intractable posterior of
$`\mathbf{z}_{n}`$ given $`\mathbf{s}_{n}`$ and $`\mathbf{v}_{n}`$, is
defined and trained jointly with the decoder
([3](#S2.E3 "In 2.2 Audio-visual VAE ‣ 2 VAE-based Speech Spectra Modeling ‣ Robust Unsupervised Audio-visual Speech EnhancementUsing a Mixture of Variational Autoencoders"))
and prior
([4](#S2.E4 "In 2.2 Audio-visual VAE ‣ 2 VAE-based Speech Spectra Modeling ‣ Robust Unsupervised Audio-visual Speech EnhancementUsing a Mixture of Variational Autoencoders"))
using a clean set of speech spectrogram frames and the corresponding
clean visual data, i.e., images of lips region \[[14](#bib.bib14)\].

Now that both A-VAE and AV-VAE are trained for their specific tasks, in
the following, we present the VAE mixture model to automatically select
one of the two at inference time.

## 3 VAE Mixture Model

To make the speech enhancement robust to noisy visual data, we propose
an automatic selection mechanism between A-VAE and AV-VAE. Ideally, such
a mechanism would allow to select the best-suited method at each frame:
when visual information is clean, AV-VAE, and otherwise, A-VAE. We
formalise this with a mixture model, named VAE mixture model (VAE-MM):

|     |                                 |                                                                                                                                                          |                                                                                            |     |
| --- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle p(s\_{fn}       | {\bf z}_{n},{\mathbf{v}}_{n},\alpha\_{n})`$                                                                                                              | $`\displaystyle=\Big[{\cal N}_{c}(0,\sigma_{f}^{a}({\bf z}_{n}))\Big]^{\alpha_{n}}\times`$ |     | (5) |
|     |                                 | $`\displaystyle\phantom{{}=1}\Big[{\cal N}_{c}\left(0,\sigma_{f}^{av}({\bf z}_{n},{\mathbf{v}}_{n})\right)\Big]^{1-\alpha_{n}},`$                        |                                                                                            |     |
|     | $`\displaystyle p({\bf z}\_{n}  | {\mathbf{v}}_{n},\alpha_{n})`$                                                                                                                           | $`\displaystyle=\Big[{\cal N}(\boldsymbol{0},{\bf I})\Big]^{\alpha_{n}}\times`$            |     | (6) |
|     |                                 | $`\displaystyle\phantom{{}=1}\Big[\prod_{l}{\cal N}\left(\bar{\mu}_{l}({\mathbf{v}}_{n}),\bar{\sigma}_{l}({\mathbf{v}}_{n})\right)\Big]^{1-\alpha_{n}}`$ |                                                                                            |     |
|     | $`\displaystyle p(\alpha_{n})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\pi^{\alpha_{n}}\times(1-\pi)^{1-\alpha_{n}},`$                                                                                          |                                                                                            | (7) |

where, $`\alpha_{n}\in\left\{0,1\right\}`$ is a latent variable
specifying the component of the mixture model that is used by the
$`n`$-th frame, for both $`s_{fn}`$ and $`{\bf z}_{n}`$. The prior
distribution of $`\alpha_{n}`$ is chosen as a Bernoulli distribution
with parameter $`\pi`$.

## 4 VAE-MM Inference & Learning

The observed noisy microphone signal writes:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

       ``` math
       x_{fn}=s_{fn}+b_{fn},
       ```                    |     | (8) |

for all TF bins $`(f,n)`$. Similarly as done in the previous
works \[[5](#bib.bib5), [6](#bib.bib6), [7](#bib.bib7), [8](#bib.bib8),
[9](#bib.bib9), [10](#bib.bib10)\], we use an unsupervised NMF-based
Gaussian noise model that assumes independence across TF bins:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
b_{fn}\sim\mathcal{N}_{c}\left(0,\left(\mathbf{W}_{b}\mathbf{H}_{b}\right)_{fn}\right),
``` |  | (9) |

where $`\mathbf{W}_{b}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{F\times K}`$ is a nonnegative
matrix of spectral power patterns and
$`\mathbf{H}_{b}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{K\times N}`$ is a nonnegative matrix of
temporal activations, with $`K`$ such that $`K(F+N)\ll FN`$.

The set of parameters to be estimated is defined as
$`\Theta=\left\{\mathbf{W}_{b},\mathbf{H}_{b},\pi\right\}`$. We use a
variational expectation-maximization (VEM) approach \[[16](#bib.bib16)\]
to estimate these parameters. To do so, the intractable posterior
$`p({\bf s}_{n},{\bf z}_{n},\alpha_{n}|{\bf x}_{n})`$ is approximated by
a variational distribution factorizing as follows:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
r({\bf s}_{n},{\bf z}_{n},\alpha_{n})=r({\bf s}_{n})\;r({\bf z}_{n})\;r(\alpha_{n}).
``` |  | (10) |

The variational factors in
([10](#S4.E10 "In 4 VAE-MM Inference & Learning ‣ Robust Unsupervised Audio-visual Speech EnhancementUsing a Mixture of Variational Autoencoders"))
are then estimated by minimizing the Kullback-Leibler divergence between
([10](#S4.E10 "In 4 VAE-MM Inference & Learning ‣ Robust Unsupervised Audio-visual Speech EnhancementUsing a Mixture of Variational Autoencoders"))
and the true posterior. The final update formulas for the variational
distributions are given below and are used in an alternating
optimization strategy. The details of the derivations are provided in a
supporting document which is available online.¹¹ 1
https://team.inria.fr/perception/research/vae-mm-se/

### 4.1 E-$`{\bf s}_{n}`$ step

The variational distribution of $`{\bf s}_{n}`$ factorizes over $`f`$.
For each component, we obtain the following:

|     |                                          |     |      |
|-----|------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       r(s_{fn})={\cal N}_{c}(m_{fn},\nu_{fn}),
       ```                                       |     | (11) |

where

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\begin{cases}m_{fn}&=\frac{\gamma_{fn}}{\gamma_{fn}+\left(\mathbf{W}_{b}\mathbf{H}_{b}\right)_{fn}}\cdot x_{fn}\\
\nu_{fn}&=\frac{\gamma_{fn}\cdot\left(\mathbf{W}_{b}\mathbf{H}_{b}\right)_{fn}}{\gamma_{fn}+\left(\mathbf{W}_{b}\mathbf{H}_{b}\right)_{fn}}\end{cases},
``` |  | (12) |

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\gamma_{fn}^{-1}=\sum_{\alpha_{n}\in\left\{0,1\right\}}r(\alpha_{n})\cdot\eta_{fn}^{\alpha_{n}},
``` |  | (13) |

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\eta_{fn}^{\alpha_{n}}=\mathbb{E}_{r({\bf z}_{n})}\left[\frac{1}{\sigma_{f}^{\alpha_{n}}({\bf z}_{n},{\mathbf{v}}_{n})}\right]\approx\frac{1}{D}\sum_{d=1}^{D}\frac{1}{\sigma_{f}^{\alpha_{n}}({\bf z}_{n}^{(d)},{\mathbf{v}}_{n})},
``` |  | (14) |

and $`\left\{{\bf z}_{n}^{(d)}\right\}_{d=1}^{D}`$ is a sequence sampled
from $`r({\bf z}_{n})`$. Moreover, we have defined
$`\sigma_{f}^{\alpha_{n}}({\bf z}_{n},{\mathbf{v}}_{n})`$ as follows:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sigma_{f}^{\alpha_{n}}({\bf z}_{n},{\mathbf{v}}_{n})=\begin{cases}\sigma_{f}^{a}({\bf z}_{n})&\alpha_{n}=1\\
\sigma_{f}^{av}({\bf z}_{n},{\mathbf{v}}_{n})&\alpha_{n}=0\end{cases}.
``` |  | (15) |

### 4.2 E-$`{\bf z}_{n}`$ step

For $`r({\bf z}_{n})`$ we obtain the following result:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
r({\bf z}_{n})\propto\exp\Big(\sum_{\alpha_{n}\in\left\{0,1\right\}}r(\alpha_{n})\cdot\Big[\log p({\bf z}_{n}|{\mathbf{v}}_{n},\alpha_{n})+\\
\sum_{f}-\log\Big({\sigma_{f}^{\alpha_{n}}({\bf z}_{n},{\mathbf{v}}_{n})}\Big)-\frac{|m_{fn}|^{2}+\nu_{fn}}{\sigma_{f}^{\alpha_{n}}({\bf z}_{n},{\mathbf{v}}_{n})}\Big]\Big).
``` |  | (16) |

The above distribution cannot be computed in closed-from. Nevertheless,
we can draw samples from it using the Metropolis-Hastings (MH) algorithm
\[[16](#bib.bib16)\].

Let $`\tilde{r}({\bf z}_{n})`$ denote the right-hand side of
([16](#S4.E16 "In 4.2 E-𝐳_𝑛 step ‣ 4 VAE-MM Inference & Learning ‣ Robust Unsupervised Audio-visual Speech EnhancementUsing a Mixture of Variational Autoencoders")).
At the iteration $`m`$ of the MH algorithm, given a current sample
$`{\bf z}_{n}^{(m-1)}`$, to obtain the next one, i.e.,
$`{\bf z}_{n}^{(m)}`$, we first draw a candidate sample from a proposal
Gaussian distribution centered around $`{\bf z}_{n}^{(m-1)}`$ and with
$`\epsilon{\bf I}`$ as the covariance matrix. The candidate sample,
denoted $`{\bf z}_{n}^{(T)}`$, is accepted by the probability
$`p=\min\Big(1,\frac{\tilde{r}({\bf z}_{n}^{(T)})}{\tilde{r}({\bf z}_{n}^{(m-1)})}\Big)`$.
If the sample is accepted, we set
$`{\bf z}_{n}^{(m)}={\bf z}_{n}^{(T)}`$. Otherwise,
$`{\bf z}_{n}^{(m)}={\bf z}_{n}^{(m-1)}`$. The above procedure is
repeated until the required number of samples is achieved. Furthermore,
the initial samples, obtained during the so-called burn-in period, are
discarded.

### 4.3 E-$`\alpha_{n}`$ step

To update the variational distribution of $`\alpha_{n}`$, we can write:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
r(\alpha_{n})\propto\exp\Big(\mathbb{E}_{r({\bf s}_{n})\cdot r({\bf z}_{n})}\Big[\log p({\bf s}_{n}|{\bf z}_{n},{\mathbf{v}}_{n},\alpha_{n})+\\
\log p({\bf z}_{n}|{\mathbf{v}}_{n},\alpha_{n})+\log p(\alpha_{n})\Big]\Big)
``` |  | (17) |

which is a Bernoulli distribution with

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\pi_{n}=g\Big(\mathbb{E}_{r({\bf s}_{n})\cdot r({\bf z}_{n})}\Big[\log\frac{p({\bf s}_{n},{\bf z}_{n}|{\mathbf{v}}_{n},\alpha_{n}=1)}{p({\bf s}_{n},{\bf z}_{n}|{\mathbf{v}}_{n},\alpha_{n}=0)}\Big]+\log\frac{\pi}{1-\pi}\Big)
``` |  | (18) |

as the parameter, where $`g(.)`$ denotes the sigmoid function defined as
$`g(x)=1/(1+\exp(-x))`$. The above expression is further simplified to
the following:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\pi_{n}`$ | $`\displaystyle\approx g\Big(\frac{1}{D}\sum_{d=1}^{D}\sum_{f}\log\frac{\sigma_{f}^{av}({\bf z}_{n}^{(d)},{\mathbf{v}}_{n})}{\sigma_{f}^{a}({\bf z}_{n}^{(d)})}+`$ |  | (19) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\Big(1/\sigma_{f}^{av}({\bf z}_{n}^{(d)},{\mathbf{v}}_{n})-1/\sigma_{f}^{a}({\bf z}_{n}^{(d)})\Big)\cdot\Big(|m_{fn}|^{2}+\nu_{fn}\Big)+`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\sum_{l}\log\bar{\sigma}_{l}({\mathbf{v}}_{n})+\frac{(z_{ln}^{(d)}-\bar{\mu}_{l}({\mathbf{v}}_{n}))^{2}}{2\bar{\sigma}_{l}({\mathbf{v}}_{n})}-\frac{(z_{ln}^{(d)})^{2}}{2}+\log\frac{\pi}{1-\pi}\Big)`$ |  |  |

where the expectation with respect to $`r({\bf z}_{n})`$ has been
approximated by a Monte-Carlo average.

### 4.4 M step

The parameters of the mixture model, that is,
$`\left\{\mathbf{W}_{b},\mathbf{H}_{b},\pi\right\}`$ are estimated by
optimizing the expected data log-likelihood, which takes the following
form:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
Q(\mathbf{W}_{b},\mathbf{H}_{b},\pi)=\sum_{(f,n)}-\frac{|x_{fn}-m_{fn}|^{2}+\nu_{fn}}{\left(\mathbf{W}_{b}\mathbf{H}_{b}\right)_{fn}}-\\
\log\left(\mathbf{W}_{b}\mathbf{H}_{b}\right)_{fn}+\pi_{n}\cdot\log\pi+(1-\pi_{n})\cdot\log(1-\pi).
``` |  | (20) |

The update formulas for $`\mathbf{W}_{b}`$ and $`\mathbf{H}_{b}`$ are
then obtained by using standard multiplicative update rules
\[[17](#bib.bib17)\]:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbf{H}_{b}\leftarrow\mathbf{H}_{b}\odot\frac{\mathbf{W}_{b}^{\top}\left({\mathbf{V}}\odot\left(\mathbf{W}_{b}\mathbf{H}_{b}\right)^{\odot-2}\right)}{\mathbf{W}_{b}^{\top}\left(\mathbf{W}_{b}\mathbf{H}_{b}\right)^{\odot-1}},
``` |  | (21) |

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbf{W}_{b}\leftarrow\mathbf{W}_{b}\odot\frac{\left({\mathbf{V}}\odot\left(\mathbf{W}_{b}\mathbf{H}_{b}\right)^{\odot-2}\right)\mathbf{H}_{b}^{\top}}{\left(\mathbf{W}_{b}\mathbf{H}_{b}\right)^{\odot-1}\mathbf{H}_{b}^{\top}},
``` |  | (22) |

where

|     |                                                              |     |      |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       {\mathbf{V}}=\Big[|x_{fn}-m_{fn}|^{2}+\nu_{fn}\Big]_{(f,n)}.
       ```                                                           |     | (23) |

The prior probability of $`\alpha_{n}`$ is also updated as follows:

|     |                                       |     |      |
|-----|---------------------------------------|-----|------|
|     |
       ``` math
       \pi=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\pi_{n}.
       ```                                    |     | (24) |

### 4.5 Speech enhancement

After the convergence of the VEM, the speech STFT frames are estimated
as their posterior means. That is, $`\forall(f,n)`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\hat{s}_{fn}=\mathbb{E}_{r(s_{fn})}[s_{fn}]=\frac{\gamma_{fn}}{\gamma_{fn}+\left(\mathbf{W}_{b}\mathbf{H}_{b}\right)_{fn}}\cdot x_{fn}
``` |  | (25) |

where all the involved parameters are set to their optimal values
obtained in the VEM framework.

The complete speech enhancement algorithm is summarized in
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ 5 Experiments ‣ Robust Unsupervised Audio-visual Speech EnhancementUsing a Mixture of Variational Autoencoders").
Note that by setting $`\forall n:~\pi_{n}=\pi=1`$ (respectively,
$`\forall n:~\pi_{n}=\pi=0`$), this algorithm reduces to an A-VAE
(respectively, AV-VAE) based speech enhancement method.

## 5 Experiments

Dataset and models. We considered two trained VAE models: an A-VAE and
an AV-VAE, from \[[14](#bib.bib14)\], which have been trained on the
NTCD-TIMIT dataset \[[18](#bib.bib18)\]. The test set includes about
$`1`$ hour noisy speech, along with their corresponding lips ROIs, with
six different noise types, including LR (LR), White, Cafe, Car, Babble,
and Street, with noise levels: $`\left\{-15,-10,-5,0,5,10\right\}`$ dB.

1: Inputs:

- •
  Learned A-VAE and AV-VAE models \[[14](#bib.bib14)\]
- •
  Noisy microphone STFT frames
  $`\mathbf{x}=\left\{\mathbf{x}_{n}\right\}_{n=0}^{N-1}`$
- •
  Visual embeddings
  $`\mathbf{v}=\left\{\mathbf{v}_{n}\right\}_{n=0}^{N-1}`$

2: Initialization:

- •
  Initialize the NMF noise parameters $`{\bf H}_{b}`$ and
  $`{\bf W}_{b}`$ with random nonnegative values, and set $`\pi=0.5`$.
- •
  Initialize the latent codes
  $`\mathbf{z}^{a}=\left\{\mathbf{z}_{n}^{a}\right\}_{n=0}^{N-1}`$
  (A-VAE) and
  $`\mathbf{z}^{av}=\left\{\mathbf{z}_{n}^{av}\right\}_{n=0}^{N-1}`$
  (AV-VAE) using the corresponding learned encoder networks with
  $`\mathbf{x}`$ and $`\mathbf{v}`$. Then, set
  $`\mathbf{z}=\left\{{\pi\cdot\mathbf{z}_{n}^{a}+(1-\pi)\cdot\mathbf{z}_{n}^{av}}\right\}_{n=0}^{N-1}`$.

3: while stop criterion not met do:

- •
  E-$`{\bf z}`$ step: Sample from
  ([16](#S4.E16 "In 4.2 E-𝐳_𝑛 step ‣ 4 VAE-MM Inference & Learning ‣ Robust Unsupervised Audio-visual Speech EnhancementUsing a Mixture of Variational Autoencoders"))
  by the MH algorithm.
- •
  E-$`{\bf s}`$ step: Update $`{\bf s}_{n}`$’s posterior by
  ([12](#S4.E12 "In 4.1 E-𝐬_𝑛 step ‣ 4 VAE-MM Inference & Learning ‣ Robust Unsupervised Audio-visual Speech EnhancementUsing a Mixture of Variational Autoencoders")).
- •
  E-$`\alpha`$ step: Update $`\alpha_{n}`$’s posterior by
  ([19](#S4.E19 "In 4.3 E-𝛼_𝑛 step ‣ 4 VAE-MM Inference & Learning ‣ Robust Unsupervised Audio-visual Speech EnhancementUsing a Mixture of Variational Autoencoders")).
- •
  M-step: Update the parameters using

Experimental protocol. In the following, we compare the performance of
A-VAE, AV-VAE, and the proposed VAE-MM. First, we run the three methods
on clean visual data. In the second experiment, we randomly corrupt
about $`1/3`$ of the total lips images per test instance. The occluded
images are created by randomly selecting sub-sequences of 20 consecutive
video frames and adding to the associated lips images random patches of
standard Gaussian noise. We used two standard speech enhancement scores,
i.e., the SDR (SDR) \[[19](#bib.bib19)\] and the PESQ (PESQ)
\[[20](#bib.bib20)\] scores. SDR is measured in decibels (dB) while PESQ
values lie in the interval $`[-0.5,4.5]`$ (higher the better). For
computing SDR, the mir_eval Python library was used.²² 2
https://github.com/craffel/mir_eval For each measure, we report the
difference between the output value, i.e., evaluated on the enhanced
speech signal, and the input value, i.e., evaluated on the noisy
mixture.

to A-VAE. Its performance is still better than that of A-VAE, as some of
the video frames contain clean and usable visual data.

## 6 Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed an audio-visual speech generative model based
on a VAE mixture consisting of a trained A-VAE and a trained AV-VAE.
Combined with an NMF model for noise variance \[[5](#bib.bib5),
[6](#bib.bib6), [14](#bib.bib14)\], the goal was to make audio-visual
VAE speech enhancement robust against noisy visual frames by switching
to A-VAE in an unsupervised way. We presented a variational
expectation-maximization approach to estimate the parameters of the
model as well as the clean speech. The promising performance of the
proposed method was demonstrated through some experiments.
